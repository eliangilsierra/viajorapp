Three directions for Viajorapp

Plan smarter. Travel further. Concept exploration only: every mark is hand-authored vector geometry rendered locally, with no image generator and no network access. Mock screens are concept illustrations, not product screenshots.

Recommendation: Waypoint (87.0 / 100)

Highest weighted score: it is the simplest mark, survives 16 px in colour and one colour, fills the adaptive-icon safe zone and carries the V of the name. Its weakest point is distinctiveness (route and node motifs are common in mapping), scored 3 of 5. This is a recommendation, not a decision: final brand approval belongs to the project owner, and the choice is reversible because every asset is generated behind design tokens.

Reasons per criterion
  • Recognition: Waypoint: V initial plus route-with-two-nodes is nameable in one sentence. Keyhole Road: Keyhole silhouette is read instantly; the road slot is a second, slower read. Contour: Contour lines suggest terrain, but also fingerprint or eye for some viewers.
  • Simplicity: Waypoint: Three primitives: stroked polyline, ring, disc. Keyhole Road: One disc, one rounded trapezoid, three slots. Contour: Two organic rings plus a marker; the organic curves need more thought to redraw.
  • Scalability: Waypoint: Holds at 16 px in colour and mono; origin ring hole is the first detail to close. Keyhole Road: Keyhole silhouette survives 16 px; road dashes close up at 16 px and are faint at 24 px (acceptable, they are secondary). Contour: Rings merge at 16 px and the marker disappears; needs a simplified small-size cut.
  • Distinctiveness: Waypoint: Route and node motifs are common in mapping and travel; the V-as-route is the differentiator. Lowest score of the three. Keyhole Road: Keyhole-plus-road is unusual in travel, but keyholes are heavy security/password-manager territory. Contour: Topographic motif is distinctive among general travel planners but common in outdoor and mapping apps.
  • Accessibility: Waypoint: Primary 7.7:1 and accent 4.2:1 on the light background; shape, not colour, carries the meaning. Keyhole Road: Primary 11.8:1 on the light background; the symbol is strictly one colour (dashes are cut-outs), so no accent-contrast risk. Contour: Contrast passes (6.8:1 primary); thin parallel lines are harder for low-vision users at small sizes.
  • Reproducibility: Waypoint: Directly expressible as one Compose Path plus two circles. Keyhole Road: Rounded polygon with even-odd holes; needs corner-rounding helper in Compose. Contour: Blob curves are parameterised in code, but not trivial to hand-rebuild in a vector editor.
  • Mobile suitability: Waypoint: Bold V fills the safe circle (farthest ink 306 px of the 313 px safe radius); centred under circle, squircle and rounded masks. Keyhole Road: Tall centred silhouette sits well in the safe circle; reads as a person or pawn for some viewers. Contour: Large organic shape fills the mask well but has no strong silhouette.
  • Cross-surface consistency: Waypoint: Works as favicon, store icon, README header and one-colour stamp; amber accent is the only fragile part in print. Keyhole Road: Strong in one colour; relies on the privacy metaphor, with no link to the V of the name. Contour: Fine lines suffer in embroidery, favicon and low-resolution print.

Fonts named in the type direction are open-licensed (SIL OFL 1.1 per upstream) and are not bundled here; the mock UIs fall back to system fonts. No trademark search was performed; confusion risks are the designer's judgement. Source: design/brand/tools/.
